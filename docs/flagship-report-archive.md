## Thesis

Sixteen verified transactions totaling $1.33 billion broaden the database sample, but volume remains concentrated in multifamily assets, Washington, D.C., and five large sales.

## Summary

An expanded review of 16 source-linked Mid-Atlantic commercial property sales across 2024–2026, examining sector mix, geographic concentration, pricing coverage, and the limits of a selective transaction sample.

## Scope of the expanded monitor

This second edition reviews all 16 verified asset sales stored in the Mid-Atlantic CRE Intelligence database as of August 8, 2026. The transactions are dated from July 29, 2024 through May 22, 2026 and cover selected properties in Maryland, Washington, D.C., and Northern Virginia. Together they represent $1,328,736,001 in recorded consideration. The median sale price is $54,012,500.

The larger sample supports a more useful view of sector and geographic concentration than the first six-transaction edition. It still does not represent every regional sale, and it should not be interpreted as a market-wide index, a complete sales-volume series, or an appraisal benchmark.

## A larger sample remains concentrated in a few transactions

The five largest sales account for $840.1 million, or 63.2% of all recorded volume. Those transactions are 8001 Woodmont at $194 million, the retail portion of Annapolis Town Center at $187 million, WestEnd25 at $186 million, The Batley at $155 million, and 1401 H Street NW at $118.086 million.

The difference between the $54.0 million median and the five largest transactions shows why aggregate volume needs to be read with the underlying records. Adding or removing one large multifamily or retail transaction would materially change the total even though the number of observations would barely move.

## Office leads transaction count while multifamily leads volume

Office properties represent eight of the 16 transactions, exactly half of the sample. Their combined $359.711 million equals 27.1% of total recorded volume. The office records range from the $10.325 million sale of 2101 E Jefferson Street to the $118.086 million sale of 1401 H Street NW.

Multifamily contains only three transactions, but their combined $535 million represents 40.3% of total volume. Retail contributes two transactions and $245.025 million, or 18.4%. The remaining hotel, industrial, and medical-office observations combine for $189 million, or 14.2%.

The comparison reinforces a central research point: transaction count and dollar volume answer different questions. Office appears most frequently, while multifamily contributes the largest share of consideration because each of its three recorded sales is large.

## Washington, D.C. is the sample's geographic center

Seven Washington, D.C. transactions total $684.886 million, equal to 51.5% of the sample. Maryland contributes five transactions and $476.325 million, or 35.8%, led by sales in Bethesda and Annapolis. Four Northern Virginia transactions total $167.525 million, or 12.6%.

These shares describe the current database, not the relative size or liquidity of the three regional markets. Collection depth is uneven by jurisdiction, and a future batch of county records could change the geographic distribution materially.

## Price per square foot is available for 14 records

Fourteen transactions have both a recorded sale price and a usable building-area figure. Their calculated price per square foot ranges from approximately $47 to $681, with a cross-sector median of about $298.

Two records remain outside that calculation. The Westin Washington lacks a verified building-area figure, while The Batley is withheld because public sources use materially different area conventions. No missing value was estimated. More importantly, the 14 usable observations combine office, retail, industrial, multifamily, and medical-office assets with different income profiles, conditions, locations, area conventions, and operating structures. The median is descriptive of this sample only and is not a valuation benchmark.

## The calendar distribution does not establish a trend

Fourteen of the 16 transactions occurred in 2025 and account for $1.249 billion, or 94.0% of total sample volume. The database contains one observation from 2024 and one from 2026.

That distribution reflects the timing of the records collected so far. It cannot support a year-over-year conclusion about liquidity, pricing, or transaction velocity. A credible time-series comparison will require a more balanced record count across multiple full years.

## Cap-rate evidence remains insufficient

Only two records contain usable reported capitalization rates. The market dashboard therefore continues to suppress the median and distribution because the minimum publication threshold is three observations.

The unavailable result is itself informative. Public transaction announcements often disclose price and parties while omitting net operating income and cap rate. Publishing a statistic from two cross-sector observations would create more apparent precision than the evidence supports.

## What the expanded dataset supports today

The 16-record sample can now show where recorded volume is concentrated, how sector rankings change between transaction count and dollar value, which markets currently dominate the collected evidence, and where missing fields constrain comparison. Each conclusion can be traced back to an individual property page and its attached sources.

The next research priority is balance rather than scale alone. Additional records should expand industrial, hotel, medical-office, and retail coverage; improve year-by-year continuity; and add reported income data only when credible public evidence exists. Until that work is complete, the database is best treated as a transparent collection of verified transactions rather than a comprehensive market index.

## Limitations

- The database contains 16 selected transactions and does not represent every sale in the region.
- The records span multiple property sectors, jurisdictions, and dates, limiting direct comparability.
- Fourteen of 16 records occurred in 2025, so the sample cannot support a balanced year-over-year trend.
- Price per square foot is available for 14 records. One building-area value is unavailable and one is withheld because public sources use non-comparable area conventions; neither was estimated.
- The cross-sector price-per-square-foot median is descriptive and is not a valuation benchmark.
- Cap-rate statistics are withheld because only two usable reported observations are available.
- Public records and company disclosures may be delayed, revised, or incomplete.
- This report is for educational and research purposes and is not investment advice.
